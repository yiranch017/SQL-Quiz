## Q2 · JOIN · WHERE · 4 points

Return the first_name, last_name and job_title of every Full-time employee in the Sales department who has a performance_rating of 4.0 or higher.

Row order matters: **no**

```sql
SELECT e.first_name, e.last_name, e.job_title
FROM employees e
JOIN departments d ON d.id = e.department_id
WHERE d.name = 'Sales'
  AND e.employment_type = 'Full-time'
```

## Q3 · GROUP BY · aggregates · 4 points

For each employment_type, return the type, the number of employees (headcount) and the average salary rounded to a whole dollar (avg_salary).

Row order matters: **no**

```sql
SELECT employment_type,
       COUNT(*) AS headcount,
       ROUND(AVG(salary), 0) AS avg_salary
FROM employees
GROUP BY employment_type;
```

## Q4 · JOIN · GROUP BY · HAVING · 4 points

Return each department name and its average salary rounded to 2 decimals (avg_salary), but only for departments whose average salary is above 80000. Show the highest average first.

Row order matters: **yes**

```sql
SELECT d.name, ROUND(AVG(e.salary), 2) AS avg_salary
FROM employees e
JOIN departments d ON d.id = e.department_id
GROUP BY d.name
HAVING AVG(e.salary) > 80000
ORDER BY avg_salary DESC;
```

| name | avg_salary |
|---|---|
| Engineering | 121250.00 |
| Data Science | 112954.55 |
| Sales | 84208.33 |

## Q5 · Subquery · correlated · 4 points

For each department name, count how many of its employees earn more than the average salary of their own department (employees_above_avg). Departments with none may be left out.

Row order matters: **no**

```sql
SELECT d.name, COUNT(*) AS employees_above_avg
FROM employees e
JOIN departments d ON d.id = e.department_id
WHERE e.salary > (SELECT AVG(x.salary)
                  FROM employees x
                  WHERE x.department_id = e.department_id)
GROUP BY d.name;
```
